Quantum entanglement is a physical phenomenon that occurs when a group of particles are generated, interact, or share spatial proximity in a way such that the quantum state of each particle cannot be described independently of the state of the others, even when the particles are separated by a large distance. Measuring one entangled particle immediately determines the correlated state of its partner, a feature that Einstein famously called "spooky action at a distance."

Superposition refers to the principle that a quantum system can exist in multiple states simultaneously until it is measured. Upon measurement, the superposition collapses into a single definite state.

The Bell inequality is a mathematical inequality derived by John Stewart Bell. Testing the Bell inequality experimentally allows physicists to distinguish quantum mechanical predictions from those of local hidden variable theories, and experiments confirm quantum mechanics violates the inequality.
